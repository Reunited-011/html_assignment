West African Jollof Rice

Jollof rice is a beloved one-pot dish celebrated across West Africa for its smoky flavor, vibrant red color, and rich blend of spices. Perfect for parties, family gatherings, or Sunday dinners, this recipe guides you through creating a traditional party-style Jollof rice.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice
  • 4 large fresh plum tomatoes
  • 2 red bell peppers (tatashe)
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper (atarodo, adjust to taste)
  • 1 large red onion (divided: half for blending, half sliced for frying)
  • 1/2 cup tomato paste
  • 3 cups chicken or turkey stock
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 2 cloves garlic and 1 thumb-sized piece of ginger (minced)
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tablespoon curry powder
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • Salt and seasoning cubes to taste

Preparation Steps

  1. Blend the pepper base: In a blender, combine the plum tomatoes, red bell peppers, scotch bonnet pepper, and half of the red onion. Blend until completely smooth, then pour into a pot and boil on medium heat until the excess water evaporates (about 15 minutes).
  2. Wash the rice: Rinse the long-grain parboiled rice thoroughly in lukewarm water until the water runs clear to remove excess starch, then drain well.
  3. Fry the tomato paste: Heat the vegetable oil in a large heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat. Add the remaining sliced onions and fry until translucent. Add the tomato paste and fry, stirring constantly for about 10–12 minutes until the bright red color deepens and the sour taste cooks out.
  4. Build the flavor base: Stir in the minced garlic, ginger, curry powder, thyme, and smoked paprika. Fry for another 2 minutes until fragrant.
  5. Combine with pepper puree: Pour in the boiled tomato-pepper mixture. Add the bay leaves, chicken stock, and seasoning cubes. Stir well and let the stew simmer for about 10 minutes.
  6. Add the rice: Add the drained rice into the simmering stew and stir gently to combine everything.
  7. Steam and cook: Cover the pot tightly with a double layer of foil paper, then place the pot lid tightly over it to trap the steam. Lower the heat to medium-low and let it cook undisturbed for 20 to 25 minutes until the liquid is absorbed and the rice is tender.
  8. Stir and serve: Remove from heat, let it rest for 5 minutes, and then use a wooden spoon or spatula to fluff and mix the rice thoroughly. Serve hot with fried plantains, coleslaw, or protein of choice.

Important Safety Notes and Warnings

Food Safety Warning: Always ensure that any leftover chicken or meat stock is brought to a strong, rolling boil before adding ingredients to prevent foodborne illness.

Burn Hazard: When the rice is simmering on low heat, never stir it too frequently, or the bottom may scorch and ruin the authentic smoky flavor.

Cooking Terms Glossary

Parboiling:
Partially boiling food as a partial cooking stage, which helps remove excess surface starch from grains like rice so they stay separate and fluffy.
Reduction:
Simmering a liquid, such as a blended tomato mixture, uncovered until its volume decreases through evaporation, concentrating its flavors.
Steaming:
A moist-heat cooking method that uses trapped steam inside a sealed pot to cook grains evenly without burning them.
